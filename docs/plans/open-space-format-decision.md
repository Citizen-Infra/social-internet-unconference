# Open space format: resolving #19, #24 and #26

**Status:** recommendation, awaiting one call from Artem · **Date:** 2026-08-25
**Resolves:** #19 (synchronous opening circle), #24 (Kaliya's format prescription), #26 (run as designed)

## These are one question, not three

The three issues were filed separately from the 2026-08-14 call, and they read as three open design problems. They are not. **#26 answers the other two.** If the decision is to run open space as it was designed and spend the novelty budget elsewhere, then the container is the tested one from #24 and the agenda is made live per #19, and neither needs deciding on its own merits.

So the only real decision is #26, and it already has converging advice from two practising facilitators who were not asked to agree with each other.

## What the practitioners settled

Tracy Kunkler and Kaliya Young gave the same advice from different angles on the same call, and Tracy repeated it in the 90-minute walkthrough on 2026-08-21. Run the vetted format first, feel what it does, then deviate knowingly. Kaliya put a number on the deviation: about 20% experimentation, 80% methods known to work.

Zicklag's counter-case on the same call still stands and is worth keeping rather than resolving — you can only ship the formula, but the idea is what has value, and people fail by getting precious about a formula that turned out not to carry the idea. The reconciliation is sequencing, not choosing a side. Run it as designed once so that the later deviation is informed rather than guessed.

## The container

From #24, Kaliya's shape, tested across four online IIW editions:

- Opening, three sessions, closing. Roughly five hours. One morning or one afternoon.
- Sessions can be three 45-minute slots rather than fewer longer ones.

Tracy's Alaska client, running this every other month, gives tighter numbers that are worth having as the operational default:

| Element | Duration |
|---|---|
| Opening: check-in plus agenda-forming | ~25 min |
| Session | 25 min |
| Transition between sessions | 2 min |
| Closing | 10 min |

Use Tracy's numbers for a first run. They come from a group doing this repeatedly rather than from a large public event, which matches where SIU actually is.

## #19 answered: the circle creates the agenda

The open question on #19 was whether the opening circle creates the agenda or ratifies what async proposals surfaced. Running the format as designed means it creates it. Kaliya's argument was that these only look equivalent: voting on sessions and making the agenda in real time differ in how they activate the field.

That leaves the async proposal round doing what IIW uses it for — social proof and pre-reading — and explicitly not producing the schedule.

## The hard constraint on automating any of it

From Tracy's 2026-08-21 session, stated twice and already recorded on #19: **the hosts decide whether their sessions are combined.** This is narrower and more useful than "do not automate the agenda":

- Software may propose merges, moves and slot suggestions.
- The named proposer accepts or rejects. Not the organisers, not a coordinator, not the system.
- This is the mechanical reason a session carries its proposer's name.

Her objection was about where power concentrates, not about ritual. Self-organising needs a period of chaos, and the chaos is how a group becomes a group. Applies equally to upvote-triggered scheduling on #13 and avails#178.

## Where the 20% goes

**Just-in-time Avails scheduling is the innovation, and it is a large one.** It is also the thing SIU exists to test. Spending the remaining novelty budget on inventing the agenda ritual, the container shape and the harvest as well is how a first run fails for reasons nobody can diagnose afterwards.

Everything above is therefore adopted as-designed, and the experiment is confined to how sessions get scheduled across participants' real availability.

## What the 2026-08-24 deferral changes

The public unconference is deferred behind a trigger (`cibc-brain/decisions/2026-08-24-community-of-practice-precedes-the-unconference.md`), so none of this is scheduled. It is not therefore idle. The community-of-practice experiment at the next CIBC town hall exercises the same mechanics with people who already know each other, and #26's advice applies to it directly: run the opening circle as designed there, at town-hall scale, before it has to work at public scale.

That makes the town hall the first test of this format decision rather than a separate track.

## The one open call

Kaliya offered two shapes and both are defensible:

1. **Single five-hour block.** Maximum energy concentration. Her stated preference, and the simpler thing to run once.
2. **Monday, Wednesday, Friday across one week.** Gets the depth that open space develops over three days while avoiding consecutive-day Zoom fatigue. Closer to SIU's scattered-scheduling instinct without abandoning the shared container.

Recommendation: **the single block for the first run.** It is the smaller thing to get right, it matches "run it as designed" better than the variant does, and the Mon/Wed/Fri shape is a deviation that should be spent knowingly later rather than on run one. The scattered-scheduling instinct is already being served by the Avails experiment; serving it twice doubles the novelty in one event.

## What stays unresolved

**Kaliya's critical mass of ~50 people for an online opening.** That is a materially higher bar than a topic clearing three upvotes, and #24 flags it correctly: if SIU cannot convene fifty people into one opening, that is an argument about the format's viability rather than a detail to route around. Nothing here resolves it, and the town-hall run will not either, since town-hall attendance is much smaller. It stays open against the public event.
