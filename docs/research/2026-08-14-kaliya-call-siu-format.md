## The headline

Two experienced open-space facilitators independently questioned the mechanic SIU is built on.

**Kaliya's argument** is that upvote-triggered auto-scheduling is not a lighter version of making the agenda live — it is a different thing, and the live ritual is what does the work.

> these things that seem like they're equivalent, like, oh, voting on sessions seems the same as making the agenda in real time. It's completely different, like in how it activates the field.

IIW does collect pre-registration signal, and then explicitly refuses to use it:

> We're very careful never to say, this is the agenda... at the event, we don't look at it. We don't upvote anything. We don't reference it.

**Tracy's argument** is narrower and more concrete. If sessions materialise from availability overlap, the open-space norm that anyone may walk into any running session has nowhere to live.

> one of the pieces of open space is, like, right, somebody could come into that call. Is that still possible?

**Conclusion on the call:** the format needs at least one synchronous anchor. Opening circle → SIU #19. Closing report-out → SIU #20.

## Format prescription

> For virtual events, I really like doing like an opening, three sessions and a closing because it creates like a solid container. It's either a morning or an afternoon. It's five hours. But if you're actually dialoguing with people, it goes by really fast.

The variant that partly answers SIU's week-spanning instinct without giving up the container:

> we had the conference happen Monday, Wednesday, Friday. So it was all contained within a week, but it wasn't three days in a row on Zoom... just like open space gets deeper over three days, it allowed that same thing to happen... we sort of prevented Zoom fatigue by spacing it out.

Critical mass online: **at least 50 people** in the opening. A much higher bar than a topic clearing three upvotes, and an argument about the format's viability rather than a detail. → SIU #24

## Run the vetted format first

Tracy:

> the first time I run a process tool, you may want to stick closer to this that works... you might want to try it as it was designed, because it's been vetted worldwide for so long.

Kaliya put a number on it:

> I'm only willing to experiment and try new made up things for about 20% of the time because I want to deliver on processes and methods that I know work.

Zicklag argued the other side from game design: you can only ship the formula, but the idea is the thing with value, and people fail by getting precious about a formula that did not carry it. The reconciliation is sequencing — run the vetted format, feel what it does, then deviate knowingly. Relevant because SIU's just-in-time scheduling **is already** the innovation. → SIU #26

## Neutral container vs org gathering

Kaliya opened with *"why do you want to put on an event?"* and pressed the question twice. On IIW:

> we are a nexus point for about, I would argue, 10 different technical communities... But IIW itself is really clear. It's just a container. So we end up being really pure in the sense that it's not our job.

> It's a question of who are you convening and what's the goal. Is this an org that is having a gathering for itself, or is it a neutral container?

This is unresolved for SIU and it shapes most of the rest. → SIU #25

## The recording norm, unanswered

Nicolas Gimenez, drawing on DICE (which he calls "the IIW of Europe", attended twice):

> when you convene a session, it is not filmed, it is not recorded, you only take manual notes, and nobody is supposed to videotape the conference itself... And so my question to you, Kaliya, and to anyone that is knowledgeable in the un-conference is, how do you transfer this into the [online format]

The question did not get answered on the call and remains open. It bounds what SIU should capture: notes in an Etherpad and a transcript are different artefacts, and the difference is part of why people speak freely. → SIU #23

## Closing circle has three movements

Not one. Report-outs of session titles by whoever convened them; then an open floor for cross-session synthesis; then **open gifting** — appreciations for work done in the life of the community, or for a conversation that helped. Ran virtually through the pandemic. The first two harvest information; the third does relational work. → SIU #20

## QiQo as prior art

QiQo Chat ran IIW virtually for 400+ paying attendees at regular prices, built by a two-person shop. Avatar-based spatial navigation with pluggable video backends (Zoom, BigBlueButton, WeChat). Other features that matter to SIU:

- **Circles / spaces** — repeat events nest under a parent circle, and organisers clone a past event's back end as the next one's starting point.
- **Privacy-preserving directory** — attendees can contact each other without email addresses being handed over, and choose whether to respond.
- **Magic-link entry, no accounts** — a personalised link in an email gets you in without logging in.

Its own identity model is email by design. Adoption candidate rather than competitor. → SIU #18
