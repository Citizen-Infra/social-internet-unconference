# Kaliya Young QiQo Walkthrough Brief

**Prepared:** 2026-08-13
**Purpose:** Use Kaliya's walkthrough of IIW and other online-only
unconferences to decide SIU's synchronous Open Space layout.
**Ask:** Show the participant journey and operating roles, including what
worked poorly. This is a comparative review, not a request to recommend or sell
QiQo.

## Decisions We Need From The Walkthrough

1. What is SIU's equivalent of the physical opening circle and agenda
   marketplace?
2. What single surface tells participants what is happening now, where, and
   how to move?
3. Which human roles must remain staffed throughout the event?
4. Where do session notes live during the event, and how do they enter the
   group-owned record afterward?
5. Which capabilities need one integrated event hub, and which can work across
   replaceable tools?

## Proposed Walkthrough Route

### 1. Arrival And Orientation

Please show what a participant sees from invitation link through arrival in the
opening room.

Confirm:

- whether an account or profile is required;
- how the calling question and Open Space principles are introduced;
- how participants test audio/video and learn room movement;
- how late arrivals find a person rather than only documentation; and
- what confused first-time participants at real events.

**SIU decision:** one front door and the minimum orientation needed before
participants can act confidently.

### 2. Creating The Agenda

Please replay how participants proposed topics, selected time/room slots, and
resolved collisions during online IIW.

Confirm:

- whether proposals were spoken, typed, or both;
- who could edit the agenda;
- whether everyone watched changes happen in one shared marketplace;
- how duplicates, similar topics, and overfilled time slots were handled;
- how much happened before the event versus in the opening circle; and
- when the agenda became stable enough for sessions to begin.

**SIU decision:** how Harmonica's pre-event proposals complement rather than
replace live participant agenda creation.

### 3. Moving Between Sessions

Please show how a participant went from the marketplace into a session, moved
again under the Law of Two Feet, and returned to orient themselves.

Confirm:

- whether room labels update with the current topic;
- whether movement requires opening new tabs or rejoining calls;
- what remains visible while someone is inside a room;
- how empty, full, moved, or over-running sessions appear;
- whether overflow rooms are pre-created; and
- what creates hallway-style encounters or visible social presence.

**SIU decision:** whether the agenda, lobby, and rooms can be separate tools
without imposing disorienting navigation costs.

### 4. Session Work And Notes

Please show the convener and note-taker experience inside one room.

Confirm:

- how a note-taker is recruited;
- whether each room has a pre-bound notes document;
- who can edit notes during and after the session;
- how links, unresolved questions, and next steps are captured;
- how people who attended another session contribute later; and
- what is compiled into the IIW Book of Proceedings.

**SIU decision:** the smallest live notes contract that can flow into
`unconference-brain/` after review without making the meeting platform
canonical.

### 5. Facilitation And Operations

Please identify the actual people and controls needed to operate the event.

Confirm:

- opening-circle facilitator;
- agenda-marketplace steward;
- main-room concierge for late arrivals;
- room/technical support;
- documentation steward; and
- who can rename rooms, move topics, create overflow, or recover from failure.

**SIU decision:** an explicit staffing plan, including which roles can be
combined at SIU's expected scale.

### 6. After The Event And Exit

Please show what remains available after an event and what organizers can
export.

Confirm:

- agenda export format;
- notes and participant-created content export;
- event/member roster portability;
- link persistence;
- what is lost when a QiQo circle closes or the organizer changes platform;
- whether another tool can reconstruct a usable event record; and
- which continuing-community features created value versus platform lock-in.

**SIU decision:** what QiQo can teach us without making SIU's identity,
relationships, or knowledge dependent on one event hub.

## Public Evidence To Verify With Kaliya

QiQo's own 2021 guide describes:

- pre-event social sessions in the same space so participants learn navigation;
- one main room for arrival and orientation;
- a shared room-by-time grid that participants populate with topics;
- named breakout rooms whose labels can be updated for each time slot;
- embedded Etherpad or Google Docs notes per room;
- pre-created overflow rooms;
- a volunteer remaining in the main room for late arrivals; and
- an optional post-event QiQo circle.

IIW documentation describes pre-set note forms bound to each session number and
breakout space, accessible both from the QiQo room list and agenda, then
compiled into a Book of Proceedings. Current IIW describes each day beginning
with an opening circle that creates the agenda, followed by five participant-led
working sessions.

These sources describe intended workflows. Kaliya's lived account should decide
which patterns were actually effective.

## Capture During The Call

For each mechanism, record:

- **Keep:** proven pattern SIU should preserve.
- **Adapt:** useful pattern that must be separated from QiQo or changed for SIU.
- **Avoid:** pattern that caused confusion, overload, exclusion, or lock-in.
- **Unknown:** question requiring a rehearsal rather than an opinion.

End the call with no more than three layout options and one recommended SIU
rehearsal. Do not choose a platform from a feature list alone.
